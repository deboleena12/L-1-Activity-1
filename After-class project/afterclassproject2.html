Begum Rokeya

Introduction: Begum Rokeya was a pioneering Bengali feminist, writer, and social reformer in British India. Often regarded as the mother of women's awakening in South Asia, she defied conservative societal norms to advocate for female education, establishing schools and publishing influential literature on gender equality.

Birth:

Rokeya Khatun was born in 1880 in the village of Pairaband, Rangpur (present-day Bangladesh) .She hailed from a wealthy yet strictly conservative Muslim family.

Family background:

While Begum Rokeya s father, Zahiruddin Muhammad Abu Ali Haidar Saber, was a highly multilingual zamindar, he strictly enforced the purdah system for women. He sent his sons to study at the prestigious St. Xavier's Collegein Calcutta but barred his daughters from learning any language other than Arabic and Urdu (purely for religious recitations)

Siblings:

Her elder brother, Ibrahim Saber, recognized her immense curiosity. Under the cover of night, while the rest of the household slept, he secretly taught Rokeya English and Bengali by the dim light of candles and kerosene lamps. Her elder sister, Karimunnesa, also served as an inspiration, having fought her own brief battle to learn Bengali before being married off at age 14.

Personal Tragedy:

Her marriage to Khan Bahadur Sakhawat Hossain in 1898 brought immense intellectual freedom, as he was an English-educated, progressive deputy magistrate. However, her personal life was struck by profound grief.

Advanced Philosophy and Social Critique:

Rokeya's writings were radical for her era. In her essays, she popularized the analogy that men and women are not equal are the two wheels of the same carriage. She argued that if one wheel is broken or kept smaller, the carriage of society cannot move forward.

Her popular sayings:

Rokeya is world-famous for some of her sayings. They are given below:

1.

"We think our jewelry adorns us, but in reality, these heavy gold ornaments are nothing but polished chains of slavery that we willingly wear."

2.

"Men have created rules to keep us subjugated, and they have used the name of religion to make us accept these chains quietly."

3.

"You have driven men into the Mardana (the men's quarters), just as they used to drive you into the Zenana (the women's quarters). It is only fair."

4.

"A community cannot prosper if its women are kept in ignorance. A cart cannot move forward if one of its wheels is broken."

5.

"Just as a bird cannot fly with only one wing, a nation cannot progress by keeping half of its population locked inside the house."

6.

"Education is not merely passing examinations or acquiring certificates; it is the expansion of the mind and the development of the soul."

My inspiration:

Begum Rokeya is an inspiration for my life. We should stop waiting for the perfect conditions, resources, or permission from others to grow. If we want to learn a skill, change our life, or build a future, we have the power to initiate it ourself right now, using whatever small resources we currently have. To live a truly inspired life, we should find a way to connect our personal success to the wellbeing of others. When we grow, we should pull others up with us. True greatness is measured by how many doors we open for those who come after us. Following her life, we can earn social respect and be independent.
